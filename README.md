# E-Commerce Sales & Profitability Dashboard

I analyzed two years of transaction data, covering eight product categories and four regions, to answer a set of questions about the business: which categories and regions were actually driving profitable growth, whether the company's rising discount spend was paying off, and why return rates were concentrated so heavily in one category. The goal was to recommend where to focus Q1 budget and how to reduce return-driven revenue loss.

## Problem Statement:

Leadership wanted to know which categories and regions were actually driving profitable growth (not just revenue), whether the company's rising discount spend was paying off, and why return rates were concentrated so heavily in one category.Also to recommend where to focus Q1 budget and how to reduce return-driven revenue loss.

## Dataset:

- ~12,800 transaction line items across 6,500 orders
- 1,451 unique customers
- 8 product categories: Electronics, Clothing, Home & Kitchen, Beauty & Personal Care, Sports & Outdoors, Books, Toys & Games, Grocery
- 4 US regions: West, South, Midwest, Northeast
- Time period: January 2024 – December 2025

## Key Findings:

- **Electronics** generated the largest share of revenue but carried the weakest profit margin in the catalog (high volume wasn't converting into meaningful profit).
- **Clothing's return rate** was nearly double the store-wide average, consistent with sizing and fit issues common in apparel.
- **Discount levels were consistent (~10%) across all categories**, which ruled out discount depth as the cause of Electronics' weak margins and pointed instead to its underlying cost structure.
- **Paid Search and Organic Search** delivered the highest average order value among all marketing channels.

## Recommendations:

1. Review Electronics' pricing and cost structure to close the gap between revenue and profit.
2. Add size guides to Clothing product pages to reduce return-driven revenue loss.
3. Reallocate Q1 marketing budget toward higher-performing acquisition channels (Paid/Organic Search).
4. Deprioritize investment in categories currently operating at a loss (Grocery).
